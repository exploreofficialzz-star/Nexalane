# NEXALANE — GAME DESIGN BIBLE

## 1. Product promise
NEXALANE turns the familiar one-thumb endless-runner format into a cinematic route-choice game where the player repeatedly decides how much risk to take.

## 2. Design pillars
1. Instant readability.
2. Satisfying movement.
3. Meaningful route decisions.
4. Skill-driven FLOW.
5. Visible progression.
6. Constant novelty without chaos.
7. Respectful monetization.
8. A world worth returning to.

## 3. Run anatomy
A standard run should contain:
- 5–10 sec onboarding/settling segment;
- early confidence segment;
- first meaningful route fork;
- collection/near-miss opportunity;
- first Flow threshold;
- environmental event;
- second route fork;
- rising speed;
- pursuit escalation;
- high-risk reward corridor;
- recap/death/reward loop.

## 4. Camera
Third-person chase camera with subtle forward look-ahead, lane-aware framing, controlled FOV expansion at high speed, minimal shake by default, cinematic preset only during scripted set pieces.

## 5. Feel
Movement must have:
- immediate input response;
- clear anticipation;
- short recovery window;
- consistent jump arc;
- predictable slide capsule;
- readable collision margins.

## 6. Score
Score = distance score + collection score + stunt score + Flow multiplier + route bonuses + challenge bonuses.
Avoid runaway inflation; normalize values for display where appropriate.

## 7. Reward design
Every run should answer three questions:
- What did I earn?
- What did I progress?
- What am I doing next?

## 8. Mastery
Mastery tracks skill and collection separately.
Skill mastery:
- no-hit distance;
- near misses;
- perfect moves;
- Flow uptime;
- advanced routes.
Collection mastery:
- runners;
- cosmetics;
- districts;
- achievements;
- lore.

## 9. District identity
Old Quarter — dense market alleys, warm stone, signage, scooters.
Transit Core — trains, stations, switch yards, glass and steel.
Harbor Arc — containers, cranes, water, bridges, ship lights.
Industrial Belt — factories, pipes, forklifts, steam, sparks.
Skyline Works — construction, towers, cranes, suspended paths.
Neon Market — dense nightlife, holographic signage, crowds.
Stormline — rain, lightning, wet surfaces, emergency lighting.
Central Spire — premium glass, elevated transit, corporate megastructure.

## 10. Environmental storytelling
Place collectible lore fragments, posters, overheard radio snippets and visual clues to explain the world without interrupting the run.

## 11. Set pieces
Set pieces should be memorable and reusable in marketing footage:
- train split;
- rooftop collapse;
- crane swing;
- bridge sprint;
- drone swarm;
- blackout transition;
- storm surge;
- central spire ascent.

## 12. Daily/weekly habit
Daily: short clear goals.
Weekly: mastery and ranking.
Seasonal: new content and collection.
Long-term: runner roster, district completion, cosmetics and records.

## 13. Ethical engagement rules
The game should be compelling because of play, mastery, collection and social comparison — not because of deceptive pressure, hidden timers, forced purchases or manipulative punishment.
